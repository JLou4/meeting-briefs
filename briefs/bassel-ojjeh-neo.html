What He Actually Thinks

His breakout piece — "Is YC for Cowards?" (Feb 2026) — is the fastest way to read him. Core arguments:

  • Stanford traded courage for optimization. Admits a "manicured intelligence" — the student-body-president / valedictorian / app-builder archetype — who avoids true risk and optimizes for defensible, dinner-table-approved outcomes.
  • YC has become a "prestige-hedged risk." The "YC Founder" badge caps the social downside even on failure, so applying is "entrepreneurship sanitized… a risk for cowards." Notes batches are now ~88% AI and diluted vs. YC's once-contrarian roots.
  • Campus clubs = résumé badges. Coins the "boba mafia" (BASES / ASES / Stanford Finance / Consulting) as alphabet-soup credential mills, not conviction-builders.
  • Real courage is un-optimized. "Do something you can't explain at Thanksgiving." Legitimacy is what happens *after* you win, not a permission slip you collect first.
Signal: He's deliberately contrarian, writes well, and enjoys the fight. The essay was strong enough that the co-president of BASES publicly conceded he was right and a VC wrote a full rebuttal. For an underclassman, that's real reach — and a tell that he thinks in provocations, not platitudes.
