Short version: David runs private equity for one of the oldest Saudi family investment groups. He came up running direct deals in North and South America[2]. Olayan pitches itself as "long-term, patient capital… supporting our companies without any real pressure to sell", which is his own quote[3]. Most of his deals are minority or co-control stakes alongside a lead sponsor, not seed. Your best opener is Emerald AI (Aug 2026), where Olayan joined the $150M Series A of a company that makes AI data centers flexible loads for the power grid[4][5]. That sits right on TBC's AI × energy thesis.

Recent Deals to Bring Up

Olayan announces very few deals publicly. Its own news page hasn't listed a PE transaction since PurposeBuilt Brands in 2022[8]. The 2025–26 items below come from the companies' or partners' announcements. Ask him what the Americas directs team has done lately; there's probably more than is public.

1. Emerald AI: Series A, Aug 2026 ⭐ lead with this

  • $150M oversubscribed Series A at a $1.05B valuation, co-led by Energize Capital and DCVC. The Olayan Group participated alongside Nvidia, Siemens, Samsung Ventures, Aramco Ventures, RWE, JERA, Lowercarbon, John Doerr and others[4][5]
  • Its Emerald Conductor software throttles and shifts AI workloads so a data center's power draw drops when the grid is stressed. It launched the first US "Flexible Load Interconnection Program" with Silicon Valley Power[5]
  • Why it matters for you: it's the commercial answer to the grid-capacity problem in your PJM / "power the chips" work. Ask whether PE made this investment or another team did, and whether venture-stage AI infrastructure is a new lane for Olayan.

2. MS Pharma: minority growth equity, May 2026

  • Olayan Financing Company (OFC, the Riyadh arm) bought a minority stake in Jordan-based MS Pharma by subscribing for new shares, to fund biosimilars, manufacturing and Saudi/MENA expansion[9][10]
  • Its biologics site was the first in Saudi Arabia to receive SFDA GMP approval for biologics manufacturing[9]. Fits Olayan's "Health & Wellness" focus[3]. This is an OFC deal, so probably not David's own, but it's the group's most recent announced PE deal.

3. Weir Group JV: Saudi mining technology, 2025–26

  • Olayan Saudi Holding and Weir announced a joint venture to sell mining technology into Saudi Arabia's growing mining sector (Jan 2025)[11]. It's a strategic operating partnership rather than PE. Useful context for how the Saudi side works.

4. Digital infrastructure & energy transition (his Americas patch)

  • Scala Data Centers (Latin America hyperscale): Olayan is a named investor alongside DigitalBridge, IFC, IMCO and Coatue[12]
  • Westwood: David sits on the board of Westwood Parent, Inc.[2]. That entity filed Form Ds in 2021, 2023 and 2024 from a Minnetonka, MN address[13]. It appears to be the holding company of Westwood Professional Services, a renewables and power engineering firm in which Blackstone Energy Transition Partners took a majority stake in Aug 2024[14]. Unconfirmed link; ask whether Olayan kept a stake.
  • Andean Tower Partners (LatAm towers): Olayan was one of the backers of ATP's Torres Unidas acquisition[15]. David was on the board[2]

5. The core portfolio, per Olayan

  • PurposeBuilt Brands (2022): Olayan and Rock Mountain Capital bought 48% from Carlyle. Olayan now co-controls the company with TA Associates. Specialty cleaning products, with 41 straight years of growth as of 2021[3][16]
  • Stone Canyon Industries Holdings: Olayan was among the investors in its $1.8B equity raise (2019). David is on the board[17][2]
  • Novotech (2022, pre-IPO alongside TPG Asia). CloudPay (led Series D in 2021 plus a 2022 follow-on). Wireless Logic (2021, with Montagu). Asurion (held since 2012)[3]
  • Accurus Aerospace: a former board seat of David's[2]. KKR reportedly sold Accurus to another PE buyer in early 2026, for roughly $1.5B in gross proceeds to KKR[18]. Olayan's role in that exit isn't public; ask.

The Olayan Group: Context

  • Family-owned. The parent company is in Vaduz, Liechtenstein, with offices in New York, London, Athens, Singapore and Riyadh/Al-Khobar (OFC)[1]
  • Chair: Hutham Olayan. CEO since Jan 2024: Hani Lazkani, previously Global Head of Alternative Investments, who opened the Singapore office[19]. CFO Musa Zaghloul since Jan 1, 2026[8]
  • David's peers: Ziad Haddad (Global Head of PE Funds) and Hala Zeibak (Head of PE Direct, Europe)[3]
  • Direct focus sectors: Health & Wellness, Enterprise Software & Tech-Enabled Services, Business Services & Niche Manufacturing, Energy & Energy Transition, Digital Infrastructure[3]

Questions to Ask

  1. Emerald AI: how did Olayan get into that round, and does it signal a bigger push into AI × power infrastructure?
  2. What has the Americas directs team done in 2025–26 that isn't public?
  3. How do they think about the chain from power to data centers to the grid (Scala, Westwood, Emerald)? Where do they think the bottleneck is?
  4. "No pressure to sell": what does a real hold period look like, and how do they manage exits with co-investors (e.g. Accurus)?
  5. Do they ever go earlier, or do they co-invest with emerging managers? The funds side (Ziad Haddad's team) has three decades of LP relationships[3].
