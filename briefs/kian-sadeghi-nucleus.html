Nucleus: The Company

Product evolution

  • 2021–22: $3.5M seed for a consumer genetic test[8]. Alexis Ohanian (776) backed it early[9].
  • 2024: Launched Nucleus Health (saliva-based whole-genome sequencing for adults, Mar 2024) and advanced carrier screening (Aug 2024)[5]. Also Nucleus IQ, which correlates a user's genetics with intelligence; this started the controversy[1].
  • Jan 2025: $14M Series A led by Founders Fund and Seven Seven Six, with Neo, Balaji Srinivasan and others. Total raised ~$32M[1][3]. Acquired Cambrean (Irish AI health-data / wearables platform)[5]. Samsung Next later made a strategic investment[3].
  • Jun 2025: Nucleus Embryo launched, built on a partnership with Genomic Prediction. It scores IVF embryos for 2,000+ traits and conditions, including IQ, height and eye/hair color[9][7].
  • Oct 2025: "Nucleus Origin", which the company calls the first open-weight AI models for IVF and genetic optimization[10].
  • 2026: The IVF+ clinic network grew to 160+ clinics (SCRC, Fertility Institute of Hawaii, Indira IVF in India, Abu Ghosh)[2][11]. The Preview couples tool now models "100 potential children" from a couple's DNA[6]. Partnered with Legacy (at-home sperm testing) in Aug 2026[12]. Launched the Vitruvian trait models (height/BMI/IQ) in Sep 2026[7].

Where the business seems to be heading

The consumer genome test now acts as the front door, and IVF is where the money is. The pitch is selling embryo analysis plus an "all-in-one IVF experience" through partner clinics, backed by large outdoor ad campaigns ("Have Your Best Baby": NYC in Nov 2025, nationwide with IVF doctors in Sep 2026)[6]. Samsung Next's investment points to a second story: genomics as the baseline layer for wearable health data[3].

The Controversy (know this cold)

  • Science: Polygenic scores estimate relative risk across populations. They don't make precise predictions for a single embryo[9]. Several geneticists have criticized the products publicly[5].
  • The Vitruvian "14 IQ points" claim: This is the best-vs-worst spread at the top end. A read of the whitepaper puts the expected best-vs-worst gap at about 10.8 points in a batch of five, and the gain over an average embryo at about 5. Older peer-reviewed modeling puts it at about 2.5[7].
  • Regulator: On Sep 11, 2026, BBB National Programs' NAD found that Nucleus had not supported claims that IVF+ can accurately rank embryos on cancer, type 2 diabetes or autism risk, or pick the tallest or smartest embryo, or predict eye and hair color. Nucleus is appealing. It voluntarily dropped the line "see your future child's health, traits, and potential"[4].
  • Legal: Genomic Prediction (the former partner) sued Nucleus and Dr. Nathan Treff for alleged trade-secret theft after Treff joined Nucleus. The court denied GP's request for an injunction or TRO[13]. The case on the merits may still be open (not verified).
  • International: Some services are restricted in India and the UK, where selecting embryos for non-medical traits is illegal[5].
Read: The controversy isn't something that happened to Nucleus. It is the go-to-market. Every headline doubles as an ad. That works until a regulator with real teeth steps in. NAD is self-regulatory; FDA, FTC and state AGs are not.

Discussing Nucleus: Questions to Ask

  1. Revenue mix: What share is IVF+ / Embryo vs. consumer Health? What's the revenue per IVF patient, and how is it split with the clinic?
  2. The 160 clinics: How many actively send patients, versus being listed in the network? What's the attach rate per cycle?
  3. Moat: Orchid, Herasight and Genomic Prediction all sell embryo screening. Is the edge the models (training data, the 40k-sibling validation), distribution, or brand? Why did he release open-weight models?
  4. Regulatory plan: What does he do if the NAD appeal fails? How exposed is he to FDA rules on lab-developed tests, or to FTC action? What's the plan for markets where trait selection is illegal?
  5. Science vs. marketing: Ask him straight: what's the expected IQ gain choosing among 5 embryos, compared with the 14-point headline? Watch whether he engages with the question or deflects.
  6. Raise: The Series A was in Jan 2025. Is he raising now? How much, who's leading, what are the burn and runway? Why is he talking to TBC?
  7. Cambrean / Samsung: Is the wearables-plus-genome platform a real second product, or a story for strategic investors?

Fit for TBC (honest take)

  • Thesis fit is weak. TBC's core is AI, energy and deep tech. Nucleus is consumer health and fertility, sold with heavy marketing. The "AI models" framing is the only bridge, and it's thin.
  • Reputational risk is real. A TBC logo on a company the press calls "designer babies" or "eugenics" will get noticed. Iqbal / the partners should knowingly sign off on that before there's any term-sheet conversation.
  • Value of the meeting either way: Kian is plugged into the Founders Fund / Thiel network and is a sharp operator on consumer distribution. This meeting is useful for relationships and for market intelligence on reproductive tech even if TBC passes.
