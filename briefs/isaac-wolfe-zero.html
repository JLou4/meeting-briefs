Short version: Zero is trying to give ordinary households the "family office" treatment. An AI agent watches all your accounts and surfaces the money moves you'd miss, then helps you make them: tax withholding fixes, idle cash, tax-loss harvesting, unused 401(k) room, high-fee funds. Isaac brings the finance and PE side and Sree the engineering. It's a well-funded seed with a strong engineering co-founder, but it's going into one of the most crowded areas of consumer fintech.
Name check: there are several unrelated companies called "Zero" (including another Zero with a Series A in some databases). This brief covers zero.finance, the company Isaac co-founded. Also, Primary VP (NYC, which led this round) is not Principal VP (Songyee Yoon's firm).

Zero — The Product

  • Pitch: "Wealth that manages itself. Your full financial life, finally working together. Zero finds the moves that matter and makes them for you."[5]
  • Example "moves" on the site (illustrative): withholding (W-4) fix worth +$2,000/month in take-home pay; $150K idle cash moved to a Treasury fund (~$10K/yr); tax-loss harvesting on $500K of ETFs without triggering wash-sale rules; $17K of unused 401(k) room before year-end; swapping a 1.49%-fee fund for a 0.03% one[5].
  • Architecture: combines financial data connections, deterministic calculations, and AI reasoning[2]. This matters because LLMs alone shouldn't do tax math; a deterministic core is the right design.
  • Seed investors: Primary Venture Partners (lead), Inception Fund, Defiant, Greens Ventures, plus founders and operators from Lovable, Supercell, Langdock and AMD Silo AI. The Chief of Staff is Markus Vapaavuori[1]. The Nordic-heavy angel list is notable.

Questions to Ask

  1. Who's the customer? Mass-affluent tech workers with equity (Sree's profile), or broader? What's the income or asset band where Zero clearly pays off?
  2. Business model: it's free to sign up. Does it make money from subscriptions, assets under management (AUM), referral fees on products (Treasury funds, etc.), or a cut of value found? Any fee taken on recommended products creates a conflict to address.
  3. "Makes them for you": does Zero actually execute trades and moves, or only recommend them? Execution means registered-investment-adviser (RIA) or broker-dealer licensing and fiduciary duty. What's their regulatory setup?
  4. Data access: how do they connect 14+ accounts (Plaid and similar)? Equity and stock-compensation data (Carta, brokerages) and tax returns are the hard parts.
  5. Moat vs. incumbents: Wealthfront and Betterment already do tax-loss harvesting and cash sweeps. Altruist's Hazel is an AI planning agent for advisors[6]. Why does a standalone agent win against platforms that already hold the assets?
  6. Traction since the September launch: sign-ups, connected assets, and how many recommended moves users actually act on.
  7. Alpine angle: could Alpine's wealth and accounting portfolio companies be a B2B channel (white-label for advisors or CPAs)?

Watch-Fors / Fit

  • Crowded, trust-heavy category. Consumer fintech has high acquisition costs and slow trust-building, and "AI financial adviser" has many entrants. Distribution matters more than the product.
  • Regulatory surface. Personalized tax and investment advice plus execution means SEC/RIA rules, fiduciary standards and liability for wrong advice. The deterministic engine helps, but compliance is a real cost.
  • Thesis fit for TBC: moderate at best. It's applied AI in consumer fintech, not deep tech or energy. The team is strong (Harvey/NVIDIA engineering plus PE/AI operator), so it's still worth a relationship.
  • Early days. Isaac's CEO role started around Aug 2026, and there's little public traction data. Treat the website numbers as illustrative examples.
