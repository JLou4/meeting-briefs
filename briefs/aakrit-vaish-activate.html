Short version: Aakrit is one of India's best-known AI founders. He started Haptik in 2013 and sold it to Reliance Jio, then advised the government's IndiaAI Mission. Activate is his attempt to be the institutional AI investor for India: it writes checks from before a company exists through growth rounds, and it has an LP list packed with founders and VCs from the India–US corridor (Vinod Khosla, Raghu Raghuram, Bhavin Turakhia, Ritesh Agarwal, Steve Chen). For TBC, he's a valuable relationship for India AI deal flow and co-investing, even though India is outside TBC's core geography.

Activate — The Fund

Structure

  • Four strategies: Inception (first institutional check for AI × India founders), Global Growth (stakes in category-leading global AI companies with an India angle), Venture Building (incubating its own companies), and Ecosystem (content, community, events)[8].
  • Size: $105M final close after starting at a $75M target. It's one of the largest debut funds raised by an Indian founder-turned-angel, the same size as Together Fund I[1].
  • LP mix: ~60% Indian / 40% overseas in the core fund (the growth vehicles are the reverse). ~24% institutional, ~76% individuals and family offices[1].
  • Team: Pratyush Choudhury, co-founder (ex-Together Fund principal, where he led AI deals like Composio and Emergent; ex-AWS)[1][7]. Soaib Grewal, partner and head of ecosystem (ex-Times Internet)[1].
  • Perks for founders: exclusive partnerships with AI labs, with up to $1M in credits and benefits per portfolio company plus early model access[8]. Multi-year Nvidia collaboration covering Inception program onboarding, Nemotron models and co-building with Nvidia's solution architects[4].

Portfolio so far

  • Growth ($20M, fully deployed): Sarvam AI, India's sovereign-LLM company (Activate joined its ~$300M round with a strategic partnership on ecosystem, enterprise adoption and research talent)[9]; ElevenLabs; Wispr Flow[1].
  • Early-stage: 7 investments, mostly in stealth. Named: Mandrake (biotech)[1].

Thesis (in his words)

  • Two kinds of founders: (1) young, technically ambitious builders using AI in science, defence and drones; (2) experienced domain operators rebuilding legal, accounting, IT services and logistics as AI-native service companies that do the work and bill for it, rather than selling software[1].
  • His test: "Can an AI services company get over ₹1,000 crore [~$120M] in revenue only from India and surrounding markets?" He's wary of category creation and pure "US export" plays. He's less interested in yet another Indian-built enterprise agent for global clients[1].
  • On sentiment, he was worried in early 2026 that India wasn't producing compelling AI founders. Now he calls it "the best India has ever been," pointing to the Sarvam, Neysa and Emergent raises and India's AI Summit[1].

Why Jack Should Care

  1. Thesis overlap on the frontier side. His "young technical founders in science, defence and drones" bucket lines up with TBC's AI/deep-tech/defense lens. Mandrake (biotech) is an example.
  2. India deal flow and co-investing. As the first institutional check, he sees India's best AI teams before anyone else. Indian founders who go global (or base in SF) are natural co-investments.
  3. Young-founder network. This fits your goal of meeting young founders. His Inception program is literally pre-company technical teams.
  4. Market intelligence. Sovereign AI, India's compute build-out (Neysa, Sarvam), and the "AI-native services" thesis are all useful context for TBC's broader AI book.

Talking Points / Questions

  • Frontier bucket: Which science, defence or drone founders is he most excited about? How deep is India's defense-tech pipeline (and how do export controls and ITAR-style limits affect US co-investors)?
  • Co-invest: How does he think about US co-investors on India deals, and what does Activate want from a partner like TBC? (Follow-on capital, US go-to-market, US customers?)
  • Growth sleeve: ElevenLabs and Wispr Flow aren't Indian companies. What "strategic value in India" did he bring them, and is that a repeatable model?
  • AI-native services: Has anyone actually hit the ₹1,000 crore India-only test? Which category is closest (legal, accounting, IT services)?
  • Sovereign AI: His IndiaAI Mission view. Can Sarvam-style national models compete with frontier labs, or is the real game distribution and fine-tuning?
  • Haptik lessons: Building conversational AI ten years too early, then selling to Reliance. What would he do differently now?

Watch-Fors

  • Geography mismatch. Activate's core mandate is India. Most of the value for TBC is relationships, deal flow and co-investing rather than direct overlap.
  • Young fund, unproven returns. Fund I launched Dec 2025. Its 7 early-stage bets are mostly in stealth, and the growth checks went in at high valuations (Sarvam, ElevenLabs). The track record to judge is his 100+ angel deals.
  • Policy proximity. The IndiaAI Mission adviser role is a real edge (access, credibility). It also ties the fund's story to Indian government AI priorities.
