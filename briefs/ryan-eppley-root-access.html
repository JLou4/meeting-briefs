The Problem

Every machine (John Deere, Mitsubishi Electric, etc.) is assembled from hundreds of small components from different manufacturers. Integrating the firmware across all these components is a nightmare — there's no standardization, and each integration is essentially custom. The number of machines has skyrocketed but the number of firmware/embedded engineers has stayed flat.

The Solution

AI agents that automate firmware integration across the long tail of component manufacturers. Not targeting the big vendors (Siemens, Rockwell) like Nexus Intelligence — instead targeting the litany of small component makers. Fully customizable per end customer. Gives existing firmware engineers 10x more leverage.

Why It's Hard

Can't open-source integration specs — security risk (e.g., car firmware = break-in vulnerability). Each manufacturer's components are proprietary. Creates a moat but also makes the technical problem harder.

Go-to-Market

  • Primary: Big contracts with manufacturers (not per-seat SaaS)
  • Secondary: Contracts/partnerships with component vendors and systems integrators
  • Pitch to component vendors: "Help your customers integrate your components faster → you sell more components"
  • Firmware engineers sit at three places: part vendors, manufacturers, systems integrators. Root Access serves all three.
American Dynamism angle: "90% of all actuators in robots made in America are not sourced from American manufacturers." Root Access could help re-shore manufacturing by making integration with American-made components easier.

Thesis Fit

  • Robotics/Industrial Automation: Direct fit — firmware is the foundation of every robot and industrial machine
  • Defense: Supports defense, aviation, maritime embedded systems. ITAR/security implications create barriers to entry.
  • AI Infrastructure: AI agents applied to a deeply technical domain with real pain points
  • Workforce multiplier: Addresses structural shortage of firmware engineers — this doesn't get solved by training more people
