Claritype — The Company

  • Core pitch: "Trusted AI" — 100% verifiable, fact-based, auditable answers powered by their proprietary GAR (Generative Augmented Retrieval) algorithm. A single unified semantic model across enterprise systems (CRM, ERP, warehouses, custom DBs).
  • Product: a conversational AI analyst that lets non-technical users explore data without SQL or dashboards — "insights in hours, not weeks." Claims 90% adoption by non-technical users, reach across 5+ industries (healthcare, retail, government, transportation… and now energy).

The Energy Angle (why this is on Jack's radar)

Rob is actively pushing Claritype into industrial / renewable-energy operations. From the materials he's circulating:

  • Embed-and-monetize: Claritype drops into partners' existing asset-performance/control software and adds AI analysis + action features "in weeks," creating new AI revenue for the partner (a channel/OEM model, not just direct SaaS).
  • Verification-first wedge: full auditability for regulatory compliance. His explicit knock on competitors: "too-direct usage of commodity AI… that lacks the key auditing steps required for confident and compliant action." Audit trail = the moat.
  • Power-plant constraint optimizer: a renewable capital-allocation tool that generates verifiable recommendations, simulations, and back-tested economic impact. Demo numbers: a plant losing ~€80,419/yr to grid curtailment; a 10 MWh / 2 MW battery counterfactual recovers ~€204,914/yr. Sub-100ms solve times, day-by-day replay, failed-solves logged for auditability.
  • Human-in-the-loop action: an "Overwatt" microgrid copilot that recommends operational actions (start genset, open islanding breaker) and requires explicit Approve/Decline before each physical action — safety-gated, audit-friendly automation.
Framing nuance: Claritype is fundamentally a horizontal auditable-AI analytics platform that is now pointing itself at energy as a wedge vertical — not a native energy/grid company. The power-plant optimizer screens shown may be "notional applications similar to our customers' systems" (his words), so probe how much is live production deployment vs. compelling demo. Watch-Fors

  • Horizontal platform wearing an energy hat. The core product is general enterprise analytics; energy is a go-to-market motion layered on top. Make sure the energy depth is real, not a themed demo for this intro chain.
  • "Notional" demos. Rob explicitly calls some screens "notional applications similar to our customers'." Separate shipped/production from illustrative. Ask for named deployments and hard outcome metrics.
  • Seed vintage is aging. $6.6M seed dates to mid-2022. Three+ years on a seed round → either strong efficiency or a long road to PMF. Get current revenue / burn / traction.
  • "100% verifiable" is a big claim. Healthy skepticism — no LLM-based system is literally 100% correct. Understand what's actually guaranteed (retrieval provenance / audit trail) vs. marketing.
  • Multi-industry sprawl. 5+ industries at seed stage can signal either a true horizontal platform or lack of focus. Ask where they're winning repeatably.

🔍 Deep Dive — The Non-Obvious Stuff

For reading the person and the play, not just the resume. Full Claritype teardown lives in the separate deep-dive doc.

1. He's running the Palantir playbook, one layer up

Palantir's whole model is: land in a mission-critical, regulated environment; make the AI trusted and auditable enough that operators will act on it; become the system of action. Claritype is that same motion — but productized as an embeddable layer that rides inside partners' existing software instead of a heavy Palantir-style deployment. Rob isn't guessing at this market; he helped build the canonical version of it (Maven). That's the bull case: right person, right scar tissue.

2. The audit trail is the product

In energy/grid ops, the blocker to AI isn't intelligence — it's liability. No operator will let an LLM open a breaker or reallocate capital without a defensible, logged, human-approved trail. Claritype's Approve/Decline gates + logged failed-solves + verifiable recommendations are aimed exactly at that fear. If they've genuinely cracked "AI actions regulators and operators will sign off on," that's a real wedge into industrial automation. Probe how hard that audit layer actually is to replicate.

3. The tell: he's selling a wedge, testing verticals in real time

Notice the intro chain — the same energy deck routed to Norwest (Volue), Advent, and TBC, with "notional" screens. That's a founder actively hunting for the vertical + channel partner that sticks, using warm VC intros to find design partners. Read: energy is a promising thread, not yet a locked-in franchise. Your leverage — if TBC has real energy-operator relationships (IPPs, renewable asset owners, grid-software vendors), you're not just a check, you're a customer-access partner, which is worth more to him right now than money.

The read: Rob is a credible ex-Palantir-Maven founder building the "trusted, auditable AI action" layer, now pointing a strong horizontal platform at energy as its sharpest wedge. The team and the audit-first thesis are genuinely differentiated; the open questions are energy depth (production vs. notional), the channel/embed economics, and current traction on an aging seed. Go in curious and slightly skeptical: get named deployments, hard numbers, and raise status — and if you can offer energy-operator access, lead with that.
