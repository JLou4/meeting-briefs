Why Jack Should Care

  1. Top-of-funnel deal flow. EF SF is a firehose of brand-new technical founder teams. A good relationship with the person running SF is a structural edge for first look at pre-seed AI/deep-tech.
  2. She thinks like all three sides. Operator (BlaBlaCar), investor (Balderton), founder (Riff). She reads teams with unusual range and won't be impressed by pattern-matching alone — engage on substance.
  3. Co-investment / follow-on path. EF takes the earliest position; TBC can be a natural next-money partner on the standouts she's forming.
  4. Thesis alignment. EF's SF cohorts skew heavily AI / technical — squarely TBC's lane (AI, deep-tech, defense, robotics).
  5. She's a builder, not a bureaucrat. Left a comfortable VC seat to found a company, then chose to run an operation rather than coast. Values conviction and doing the hard thing.

Talking Points / Hooks

  • The EF model itself: how "talent-first / form the team, then the company" is playing out in the current AI cycle — where she's seeing the best pairs form.
  • Riff & remote work: founder-to-founder empathy; what she learned building through the remote wave, and how it shapes how she coaches founders now.
  • Balderton lens: Europe vs. SF early-stage dynamics; what transferred and what didn't when she moved west.
  • Concrete ask: how TBC can be a useful next-check partner for EF SF standouts — what's genuinely helpful to her founders at first-raise.
  • BlaBlaCar: scaling a marketplace internationally — good common ground if the talk turns operational.

Watch-Fors

  • She's a talent investor, not a fund GP writing large checks — the value is pipeline and judgment on people, not a co-invest term sheet today. Frame the relationship as long-game deal flow.
  • EF companies are very early (often pre-idea at intake). Expect raw teams; her signal is on the people, not traction.
