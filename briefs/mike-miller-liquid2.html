Who He Is

Mike Miller is a founding partner of Liquid 2 Ventures, the seed fund he launched in 2015 alongside NFL Hall-of-Famer Joe Montana and Michael Ma. Before VC, he was a particle physicist — PhD-level training and research spanning Yale, MIT, CERN, and the University of Washington — who crossed into startups by co-founding and running Cloudant, a distributed-database / DBaaS company that went through Y Combinator and was acquired by IBM in 2014. That scientist-turned-operator-turned-investor arc is the whole personality of his investing: he's fluent in hard technical bets and comfortable underwriting frontier science.

Liquid 2 Ventures — The Firm

  • Strategy: high-volume, pre-seed / seed, portfolio-driven. The explicit math ("Think Seed Like Liquid 2"): make many early bets, because a small number of 10–20x+ breakouts return the whole fund. Often a non-lead check alongside YC and other seed leads.
  • Checks: typically $250K–$500K at early stage; $2M–$5M for later (Series B+) follow-on / opportunity.
  • Scale: 800+ companies (firm's own count; databases range 668–926). Fund III was $80M, closed Dec 2021 oversubscribed vs. a $75M target.
  • Sector focus: software, cloud/infrastructure, developer tools, AI/ML, space, climate, fintech, defense/frontier-tech — deeply overlapping with TBC's thesis.
  • Notable portfolio: GitLab, Rippling, Anduril, Applied Intuition, Astranis, Solugen, Mercury, Retool, Remote, Apollo.io, Human Interest, Vetcove, Chipper Cash, Jasper AI.
  • The Montana angle: Joe Montana's brand is a real founder-magnet and top-of-funnel edge — but Mike is the technical underwriting engine of the partnership.

How He Invests

  • Founder-first, "prepared mind." Backs audacious technical teams building tech that can replace inefficient incumbents. His scientist background means he stays intellectually ready to recognize unusual technical opportunities across sectors rather than betting one narrow niche.
  • Network as the value-add. Liquid 2 leans on its founder/operator community for intros, follow-on access, and portfolio effects — the value prop for a small non-lead check is the network, not board control.
  • Volume + access, not concentration. Diversified exposure to many strong teams; the fund is built to catch outliers wherever they appear, not to run a thematic concentrated book.

🔍 Deep Dive — The Non-Obvious Stuff

For reading the person and the play, not just the resume.

1. He's the "scientist" archetype of seed investing — bet on him accordingly

Miller is not a spreadsheet-first financier; he's an experimentalist who ran real physics at CERN and then built distributed-systems infra. That means he's genuinely comfortable with technical risk and probabilistic thinking — the exact temperament a high-volume seed fund needs. For Jack, this is the opening: talk to him as a technical peer about hard-science bets (fusion, space, novel compute, energy), not as one GP pitching another. That's where the conversation gets interesting and where the relationship compounds.

2. The real product is the funnel, not the check

Liquid 2's edge is top-of-funnel access — the Montana brand + YC adjacency + 800-company network generate deal volume most seed funds can't touch. The $250–500K check is almost a tracking device to stay inside the best companies early. For TBC, the highest-value outcome of this meeting isn't a co-invest on one deal — it's plugging into that funnel as a trusted deep-tech counterparty who can take the energy/defense/robotics deals Liquid 2 sources but doesn't want to lead.

3. What to actually extract: the portfolio-math discipline

The hard, non-obvious question for a fund like this is reserves and follow-on discipline — with 800+ names, how do you decide which winners to double down on, and when? That's the make-or-break of the outlier model, and it's exactly the operational question a Fund II GP should be pressure-testing. Ask him how Liquid 2 triages its breakouts. His answer is worth more to Jack than any single intro.
